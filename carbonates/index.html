Carbonate rocks: components and classification

A limestone is made of grains (allochems), lime mud (micrite) and cement (spar). What the grains are and whether mud or grains hold the rock up give its name.

Components

Skeletal grains (bioclasts)Calcareous shells and skeletal fragments; see Fossils in thin section.
OoidsCoated grains, commonly ≤2 mm, built of concentric layers around a nucleus (a shell fragment, quartz grain or other particle). They form where waves keep grains moving, most commonly in shallow tropical seas such as the Bahamas and the Persian Gulf.
OncoidsCoated grains formed where algae precipitate calcite and storms periodically roll the grain over.
PisoidsLike ooids but larger than 2 mm; they grow in the vadose zone.
Peloids (pellets)Silt- to sand-sized grains of lime mud, mostly invertebrate fecal pellets.
IntraclastsFragments of already-lithified carbonate reworked into younger sediment, like rip-up clasts.
MicriteClay-sized microcrystalline carbonate (lime mud): the carbonate equivalent of matrix.
SparCoarse, clear calcite cement filling pore spaces.

Folk classification

Folk names have two parts: the most abundant grain type (bio-, oo-, pel-, intra-) plus the material between grains (-sparite for spar cement, -micrite for lime mud). Examples: biosparite, biomicrite, oosparite, oomicrite, pelsparite, pelmicrite, intrasparite, intramicrite; dismicrite is mud with spar-filled patches. Most useful for thin sections.

Dunham classification

Based on depositional texture: is the rock supported by mud or by grains?

MudstoneMud-supported, under 10% grains.
WackestoneMud-supported, over 10% grains.
PackstoneGrain-supported, with mud between grains.
GrainstoneGrain-supported, little or no mud.
BoundstoneComponents organically bound together during deposition.
Crystalline carbonateRecrystallization has erased the depositional fabric.

Embry & Klovan additions (coarse grains and reefs)

FloatstoneMatrix-supported, with over 10% of grains larger than 2 mm floating in finer sediment.
RudstoneThe grains larger than 2 mm support the framework.
Bafflestone, bindstone, framestoneSubdivide boundstone by how organisms bound it: baffling sediment, encrusting and binding it, or building a rigid framework.
