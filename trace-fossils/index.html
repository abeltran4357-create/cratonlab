Trace fossils

Trace fossils record what animals did (resting, crawling, feeding, dwelling and escaping) rather than their bodies. They're named as ichnogenera, which say nothing about who made them, and they're rarely transported, so they record conditions where they formed.

Trace fossils in thin section

Most trace-fossil identification is done on outcrops, cores and hand samples, but burrowing can also be seen under the petrographic microscope. In a study of Middle Jurassic mudstone, the author notes that cryptobioturbation (biodeformational structures) "is common but discernible only in thin-section," and figures its discrete micro-burrows in thin section view with a 1 mm scale bar.

Ichnofacies

Recurring groups of traces that track environment, mainly water depth, energy, oxygen and substrate. Adolf Seilacher developed the concept in 1967.

TrypanitesHardgrounds: rocky coasts, beachrock, reefs, bone beds, coquina. Borings are the only traces; bioerosion is the key process.
GlossifungitesFirmgrounds: compacted, dewatered mud or sand later exhumed by erosion, in shallow to deep water. A mix of relict and overprinted traces.
TeredolitesWoodgrounds: wood clasts, log jams, peats and coals, mostly in estuaries, delta plains and back-barrier areas.
PsilonichnusSupralittoral to upper littoral, moderate to low energy: beach backshores, dunes, washover fans, supratidal flats. Mixes terrestrial traces with marine burrowers such as crabs.
SkolithosShifting, well-sorted sand in the littoral zone under moderate to high energy: beach foreshores and shorefaces; also estuarine point bars, tidal deltas and deep-sea fan sands.
CruzianaModerate- to low-energy offshore settings: shelves and epeiric seas, and quieter parts of estuaries, bays, lagoons and tidal flats.
ZoophycosQuiet water from restricted littoral to bathyal settings, typically offshore below storm wave base, often oxygen-deficient.
NereitesBathyal to abyssal settings with slow pelagic/hemipelagic deposition, sometimes interrupted by turbidites.
Scoyenia and MermiaContinental (non-marine) ichnofacies.

Traces through time

  • The first appearance of Treptichnus pedum marks the base of the Cambrian, about 539 million years ago.
  • Cruziana and Rusophycus appear in the earliest Cambrian and persist into the Triassic, after trilobites were gone.
  • Skolithos: Early Cambrian to present. Zoophycos: Early Ordovician to present, moving from deep water (Silurian) to nearshore (Devonian–Permian) and back to deep water today.
  • Thalassinoides becomes abundant at the start of the Mesozoic; Ophiomorpha ranges from the Early Permian and is prominent from the Jurassic.
  • Paleodictyon: Precambrian/Early Cambrian to the present, in the deep sea.
