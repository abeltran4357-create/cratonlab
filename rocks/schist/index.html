Metamorphic rock

Schist

Medium to high grade, from many parent rocks (e.g., shale or basalt). Foliated, with crystals (especially mica) visible without magnification; may contain garnet, or chlorite if derived from basalt.

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Schist typically forms during regional metamorphism in mountain building, from many parent rocks, most commonly mudstone (giving mica-rich schist). It develops at elevated temperature when the rock is squeezed harder in one direction: platy minerals rotate or recrystallize into parallel layers perpendicular to the shortening direction.
