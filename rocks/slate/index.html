Metamorphic rock

Slate

Low-grade metamorphic rock from shale. Microscopic clay and mica crystals grow perpendicular to the maximum stress, giving slaty cleavage that splits into flat sheets.

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Slate usually forms by low-grade regional metamorphism of mudrock (clay or volcanic ash). Compression aligns microscopic minerals so the cleavage forms perpendicular to compression, independent of the original bedding. The rock can lose up to half its volume, and cleavage fully develops as clay converts to chlorite and mica.
