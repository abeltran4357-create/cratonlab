Igneous rock

Basalt

Mafic, extrusive and fine-grained; brown to dark green to black. Often vesicular or amygdaloidal.

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Most basalt forms by decompression melting of the upper mantle: when hot mantle rock rises, the pressure on it drops and it partly melts. This happens at mid-ocean ridges, continental rifts, hotspots and back-arc basins. At subduction zones, water released by the sinking slab lowers the melting point of the overlying mantle and adds more melting.

Source: Wikipedia: Basalt

Equivalent: Gabbro is its intrusive equivalent.
