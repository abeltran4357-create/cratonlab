Sedimentary rock

Limestone

Made of calcite and aragonite, and by far the most common biochemical sedimentary rock. It fizzes vigorously in dilute hydrochloric acid. Classify it with Folk (grains + spar or micrite) or Dunham (mud vs. grain support).

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Most limestone formed in warm, shallow marine water on continental shelves and carbonate platforms, and most calcium carbonate in the ocean is precipitated by organisms. Reefs grow at low latitudes in shallow water. Carbonate dissolves at depth, below the calcite compensation depth (4,000–7,000 m). Modern lime mud is mostly tiny aragonite needles, though how it forms is still researched. Carbonate sediment cements quickly, typically within a million years.
