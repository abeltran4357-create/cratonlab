Sedimentary rock

Travertine and tufa

Calcium carbonate deposited in fresh water, at springs, waterfalls and hot springs. Highly porous, spongy deposits are called tufa; travertine is less porous and forms at hot springs.

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Both form when carbon dioxide escapes from carbonate-rich water and calcium carbonate precipitates. Travertine forms rapidly at hot-spring mouths (CO₂ from volcanic activity) and in caves, as at Pamukkale and Mammoth Hot Springs. Tufa forms from ambient-temperature water in rivers and lakes, where microbial biofilms may be more important than chemistry alone; tufa columns form where carbonate-rich spring water enters alkaline soda lakes, as at Mono Lake.
