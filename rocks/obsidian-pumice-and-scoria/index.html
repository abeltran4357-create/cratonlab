Igneous rock

Obsidian, pumice and scoria

Glassy volcanic rocks: obsidian is smooth volcanic glass with a glassy luster; pumice is highly vesicular felsic glass that can float; scoria is highly vesicular mafic glass.

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Obsidian forms when silica-rich lava (usually 70% or more SiO₂) cools so quickly that crystals barely grow; the high viscosity also slows the movement of atoms. Over time it devitrifies into fine crystals, so obsidian older than the Miocene is rare. Pumice forms in explosive eruptions when gas bubbles in viscous magma are frozen in place by rapid cooling and depressurization; it is felsic to intermediate, never mafic. Scoria is basaltic or andesitic, erupted as molten blobs that cool in the air; it has larger vesicles and thicker walls than pumice (likely due to lower magma viscosity) and builds cinder cones.
