Sedimentary rock

Oil shale

Organic-rich, fine-grained rock containing kerogen that yields oil only when heated (pyrolysis). It is not the same as tight-oil shales like the Bakken. Organic matter comes mainly from algae, spores, pollen and plant debris, mostly in marine or lake settings; the Green River Formation (Colorado, Utah, Wyoming) is the world's largest known deposit. Kerogen types: Type 1 (algal, oil-prone), Type 2 (mixed, may produce oil), Type 3 (woody, gas-prone).

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Oil shale forms from organic matter (mainly algae, spores, pollen and plant debris) preserved in fine sediment in marine or lake settings. Its kerogen has not been heated enough to make oil; it yields oil only when heated artificially (pyrolysis).
