Igneous rock

Komatiite

Ultramafic, extrusive and fine-grained.

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Almost all komatiites formed in the Archean (4.03–2.5 billion years ago), when the mantle may have been 100–250 °C hotter. They are thought to form by very high degrees of partial melting (usually over 50%), probably in extremely hot mantle plumes or in Archean subduction zones. Their spinifex texture comes from rapid growth of olivine crystals in a temperature gradient near the top of a lava flow.

Source: Wikipedia: Komatiite

Equivalent: Peridotite is its intrusive equivalent.
