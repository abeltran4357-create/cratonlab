Igneous rock

Peridotite and dunite

Ultramafic, intrusive and coarse-grained.

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Peridotite is the main rock of the upper mantle. It reaches the surface either as mantle rock itself (fertile lherzolite, or harzburgite and dunite left behind after basaltic melt was extracted) or as cumulates where olivine crystallized and settled on the floor of a magma body. Pieces of oceanic plate emplaced onto or into continental crust (by obduction) are called ophiolites, and fragments are carried up as xenoliths from depths approaching 200 km. Surface peridotite is unstable and is often altered to serpentinite.

Source: Wikipedia: Peridotite

Equivalent: Komatiite is its volcanic equivalent.
