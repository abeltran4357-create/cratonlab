Sedimentary rock

Chalk

A soft, earthy, fine-textured limestone made of the tests of planktonic microorganisms.

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Chalk accumulated on the sea floor from calcite plates (0.5–4 µm) of planktonic coccolithophores. It was deposited on broad continental shelves 100–600 m deep, in a likely arid climate that kept land-derived sediment low. In Western Europe it formed in the Late Cretaceous and early Paleocene. Flint nodules probably come from silica of sponge spicules and other siliceous organisms, redistributed during compaction.
