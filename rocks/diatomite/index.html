Sedimentary rock

Diatomite

A soft, siliceous rock that crumbles to a fine white powder, made of the amorphous silica (opal) frustules of diatoms in lake or marine sediments; dried material is typically 80–90% silica. Light and very porous. Commercial deposits are Tertiary and Quaternary.

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Diatomite forms from silica frustules of dead diatoms accumulating in lake or marine sediment. Only a small fraction of frustules survive to burial. Lake diatomites are almost always associated with volcanic rock, and silica from volcanic ash may be needed for thick deposits. The world's largest deposit is near Lompoc, California.
