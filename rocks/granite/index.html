Igneous rock

Granite

Felsic, intrusive and coarse-grained (phaneritic). Essential quartz (over 20%) and feldspar, with alkali feldspar making up roughly 35–100% of the feldspar and plagioclase the rest.

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Felsic magmas like granite's are thought to form when heat or water is added to lower-crust rock, rather than by decompression of mantle rock as basalt does. Granitic melt can also form in place by partial melting of metamorphic rock, and rises through small, self-propagating dikes. Granites are grouped by source: I-type (high in sodium and calcium), S-type (sodium-poor and aluminum-rich), A-type (from partial melting of refractory lower-crust rock such as granulite) and M-type (sourced from crystallized mafic magma).

Source: Wikipedia: Granite

In thin section

Quartz, alkali feldspar (often microcline or perthitic orthoclase) and plagioclase, with hornblende and biotite the most common mafic minerals; muscovite is frequent.

Equivalent: Rhyolite is its fine-grained volcanic equivalent.
