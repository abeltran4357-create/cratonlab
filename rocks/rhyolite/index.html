Igneous rock

Rhyolite

Felsic, extrusive and fine-grained (aphanitic), often tan or pinkish. Can be porphyritic with quartz and potassium feldspar phenocrysts.

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Rhyolite forms when silica-rich magma erupts and cools quickly at the surface. The magma can come from differentiation of more mafic magma or from melting of crustal sedimentary rock. It is common at subduction zones, especially where the overriding plate is continental, because thick crust gives rising magma more chance to differentiate and assimilate crustal rock.

Source: Wikipedia: Rhyolite

Equivalent: Granite is its intrusive equivalent.
