Sedimentary rock

Conglomerate and breccia

Coarse-grained (clasts over 2 mm). Conglomerate has well-rounded clasts and forms in high-energy settings; breccia has angular clasts and typically forms close to the source, as on alluvial fans.

How it forms (petrogenesis)

Conglomerate forms when rounded gravel deposited by water or glaciers is buried and cemented. Gravel moves as bedload during high flow, so river conglomerates are typically well rounded but poorly sorted; alluvial fans give coarse, poorly sorted fanglomerates from debris flows; conglomerates also mark the base of marine transgressions and the bases of turbidites, and glacial tillites are poorly sorted and matrix-supported. Breccia's angular clasts point to little transport: talus at the foot of cliffs, grinding along faults, explosive volcanism, meteorite impacts, or collapse into caves and dissolved evaporite beds.
